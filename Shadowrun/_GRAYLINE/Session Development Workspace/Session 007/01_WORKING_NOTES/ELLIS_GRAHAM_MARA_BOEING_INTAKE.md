# Ellis / Graham / Mara / Boeing Intake

Status:
SESSION 007 DEVELOPMENT INTAKE

Sources:

- User-provided Ellis cover delivery structure.
- Attached corrected lock facts text.

## Core Lock Facts

- Graham is a Boeing operative.
- Mara was taken by Boeing first.
- Boeing was also trying to take Graham.
- Ellis is connected to Boeing and/or working for Boeing.
- Ellis's cousin story is a cover story.
- Ellis is not actually Graham's cousin unless later deliberately changed.
- For current purposes, the cousin relationship is false.
- Ellis is not honestly trying to help the runners communicate with Boeing.
- Ellis is trying to extract information from the runners and report it back to Boeing.

## Ellis Cover Story

Ellis claims:

- Graham is his cousin.
- Graham introduced him to Mara.
- Ellis became romantically interested in Mara.
- Ellis tried to connect with Mara.
- Mara ghosted him.
- Ellis tried Graham because Graham supervises Mara.
- Graham stopped answering.
- Ellis went to Graham's Boeing office looking for him.
- Graham was supposed to be at work.
- A Boeing corporate contact told Ellis that a dark elf had been seen around Graham's apartment.
- That contact said the dark elf was rumored to come down to the Siberian Wolf.
- Boeing told Ellis that if he found Graham, he should let them know Graham was alive.
- Ellis says this is why he came to the Wolf.

## What Ellis Pretends To Want

- Is Graham okay?
- Why did Mara ghost him?
- Why is everyone disappearing?

## What Ellis Actually Wants

- Is Graham alive?
- Who moved him?
- Was Graham sold?
- Who bought him?
- Was Ares involved?
- Was Bourgeoisie involved?
- Did the dark elf connection lead to the correct people?
- What do the runners know about Mara?
- What do the runners know about Graham's disappearance?
- How much can Boeing confirm before escalating?

## What Is True Behind The Cover

True:

- Graham is a Boeing operative.
- Mara was taken by Boeing first.
- Boeing was trying to take Graham.
- Boeing wants to know what happened to Graham.
- Boeing has traced something toward a dark elf / Wolf connection.
- Ellis is working for Boeing.
- Ellis is at the Wolf to gather information.
- Ellis wants confirmation of Graham's status and transfer chain.
- Ellis will report recovered information back to Boeing.

False:

- Ellis is honestly acting as Graham's cousin.
- Ellis is only looking for Graham out of personal concern.
- Ellis is only following a clerk's lead as a worried relative.
- Ellis will tell Boeing whatever version the runners want.
- Ellis is offering the runners control over Boeing's understanding.

## Cover Fracture

A real cousin asks:

"Is Graham alive?"

A Boeing information probe eventually asks:

"Who has him?"

That is the crack in the mask.

## Opening Version

Ellis opens with:

"I'm looking for Graham."

If asked why:

"He's family."

If asked why here:

"He introduced me to Mara. I was trying to reach her, then she stopped answering. Graham supervises her, so I tried him. He stopped answering too."

If asked how that led to the Wolf:

"I went to his office. They said he was supposed to be at work. Someone there said a dark elf had been seen around his apartment, and that the elf was known to come down here."

If pressed on Boeing:

"They did not send me as a damn courier. They told me if I found him, tell them he was alive. That is all."

If the runners show they know more:

"If he is alive, tell me that. If he was moved, tell me where. If someone bought him, tell me who."

If Ellis offers the bait:

"Give me the truth, and I can tell Boeing whatever you want them to hear."

## Pressure Version

If challenged:

"You do not have to like why I am here."

"You do not even have to believe me."

"But if Graham is alive, tell me that."

If pushed harder:

"If he was sold, tell me who bought him."

If they hesitate:

"If you give me the truth, I can tell Boeing whatever you want them to hear."

This is the trap.

## GM Note

The players may think Ellis is offering them a channel to manage Boeing.

He is not.

Ellis is collecting statements, names, reactions, and confirmations for Boeing.

The strongest bait is:

"Tell me where Graham went, and I can protect you from what Boeing thinks happened."

The actual result is:

The more they tell Ellis, the more Boeing learns.

## Session 007 Use

- Ellis should begin personal, frustrated, embarrassed, and worried.
- His story should initially sound like a messy personal errand.
- Pressure should gradually move him from "is Graham okay?" to "who has him?"
- The runners should be able to catch the shift if they listen for it.
- Ellis should not truthfully offer report control.
- If the runners feed him information, Boeing gains it.
